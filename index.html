H

Hyperliquid (HYPE)

The trading-focused one

Hyperliquid is a Layer-1 blockchain built around fast on-chain financial markets. Its best-known product is a decentralized exchange for spot and perpetual-futures trading.

What does it do?

HyperCore runs the trading system. HyperEVM lets developers build Ethereum-style apps on the same network.

Why does HYPE exist?

HYPE is used for staking/network security, HyperEVM gas and ecosystem utility. Hyperliquid's fee mechanics also create demand for HYPE through protocol mechanisms.

How was it created?

HYPE launched with a 1 billion token maximum supply at genesis. A large share was reserved for users/community rewards, with other allocations for contributors, grants and future emissions.

Bull case: Hyperliquid keeps growing into a major on-chain trading venue, activity and fees rise, and the ecosystem around HYPE expands.
Main risks: competition, regulation, contributor vesting and future token emissions. Because HYPE already has a large valuation, continued growth matters.

X

Plasma (XPL)

The stablecoin-payments one

Plasma is a Layer-1 blockchain designed around stablecoins such as USDT. Its goal is to make digital dollars useful for payments, transfers, cards and financial apps.

What does it do?

Plasma provides a settlement network for low-cost stablecoin transfers and payment-focused apps while remaining compatible with Ethereum-style applications.

Why does XPL exist?

XPL is the native asset of the Plasma network. It is part of the Proof-of-Stake security model and validator-reward system.

How was it created?

Plasma launched with 10 billion XPL. The original allocation included ecosystem/growth, team, investors and a public sale. Its design also includes validator-reward inflation.

Bull case: stablecoin usage keeps growing and Plasma becomes one of the networks actually processing meaningful payment volume.
Main risks: token unlocks and new supply can pressure price even if the product grows. Plasma also competes with many other chains handling stablecoins.
What to watchStablecoin balances, payment volume, integrations, unlocks and validator emissionsSupply idea10B initial supply plus the protocol's validator-reward design
E

Ethena (ENA)

The synthetic-dollar one

Ethena's main product is USDe. USDe is designed to behave like a digital dollar, but it is not the same as a traditional fiat-backed stablecoin such as USDC or USDT.

What does it do?

Ethena issues USDe and sUSDe. The protocol uses collateral, hedging strategies and other yield sources to support the system.

Why does ENA exist?

ENA is the governance token. Holders help govern parts of the protocol, and ENA can also be staked through Ethena's staking system.

How was it created?

ENA launched with a 15 billion maximum supply. Allocations included core contributors, investors, the foundation and ecosystem/airdrop programs, with vesting schedules for locked tokens.

Bull case: USDe becomes a much larger digital-dollar product and Ethena remains important across exchanges and DeFi.
Main risks: ENA is not USDe. ENA holders are betting on the value of the wider Ethena ecosystem. Risks include hedging, counterparties, smart contracts, funding rates, governance and regulation.
What to watchUSDe supply/adoption, protocol revenue, backing health, integrations and unlocksSupply idea15B max supply

How to think about upside

Do not judge upside by token price alone. A $0.08 token is not automatically cheaper than an $80 token. Market cap and supply matter more.

HYPE

Already the largest of the three by valuation, so large percentage gains require a much bigger network value. The upside case depends on Hyperliquid staying dominant and expanding.

XPL

Smaller valuation can mean more percentage upside, but also much more risk. Supply unlocks are especially important because new tokens can dilute price momentum.

ENA

Upside depends on USDe becoming a major digital-dollar product and ENA capturing enough value from Ethena's growth to justify a higher valuation.

Tiny glossary

Market cap

Token price × circulating supply.

Circulating supply

Tokens currently considered available in the market.

FDV

Fully Diluted Valuation: what the project would be worth if its full token supply were valued at today's price.

Token unlock

Previously locked tokens become available to move or sell.

Staking

Locking or delegating tokens to support a network or protocol, often for rewards.

Stablecoin

A crypto asset designed to stay near a reference value, usually $1.
