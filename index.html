The 30-second version

These three tokens are not doing the same thing. The easiest way to remember them is: HYPE = trading, XPL = stablecoin payments, and ENA = a synthetic-dollar system.

H
Hyperliquid
HYPE

Think: a crypto-native stock exchange + blockchain.

Hyperliquid is built around fast on-chain trading, especially perpetual futures. HYPE helps secure the network, pays network costs, can reduce trading fees, and is used as gas on HyperEVM.

TradingLayer 1Staking
X

Why does HYPE exist?

HYPE is used for staking/network security, HyperEVM gas, network costs and trading-fee discounts. Protocol trading fees also flow into community mechanisms; the Assistance Fund automatically converts fees into HYPE and burns that HYPE.

How was HYPE created?

HYPE launched in November 2024 with a maximum supply of 1 billion. About 31% was set aside for the genesis distribution to users, 38.888% for future emissions/community rewards, about 23.8% for core contributors, with smaller foundation/grant allocations. Hyperliquid says it was self-funded rather than funded by outside VC capital.

The simple bull case: If Hyperliquid keeps becoming a major place for on-chain trading, more activity can mean more demand for HYPE, more fees flowing through the ecosystem, more staking demand, and more HYPE burned.
The simple risk: HYPE already has a large valuation. It needs continued growth to justify higher prices. Contributor vesting and future community emissions can add supply, while competitors and regulation can hurt trading activity.

Plasma (XPL)

The stablecoin-payments one

Plasma is a Layer-1 blockchain designed around stablecoins â€” digital tokens such as USDT that try to stay worth $1. The goal is to make digital dollars useful for everyday payments, transfers, cards, remittances and financial apps.

What does it actually do?

Plasma is the settlement network underneath products such as Plasma One. Its docs describe low-cost stablecoin transfers, fast settlement, EVM compatibility, liquidity and payment-specific infrastructure.

Why does XPL exist?

XPL is the native asset of the Plasma blockchain. It is designed to secure the Proof-of-Stake network, facilitate network activity and reward validators. Plasma also burns base transaction fees.

How was XPL created?

Plasma launched with 10 billion XPL. The original allocation was 40% ecosystem/growth, 25% team, 25% investors and 10% public sale. When external validators and delegation activate, Plasma's published design starts validator-reward inflation at 5% per year, falling by 0.5 percentage point yearly until a 3% baseline.

The simple bull case: If stablecoins become a normal way people send and spend dollars, and Plasma becomes one of the networks that actually processes that activity, XPL could benefit from much larger network usage and demand.
The simple risk: XPL has substantial token supply still entering the market. That means Plasma can succeed as a product while XPL price still struggles if new supply grows faster than demand. It also competes with many chains that already handle stablecoins.

Ethena (ENA)

The synthetic-dollar one

Ethena's main product is USDe. USDe aims to behave like a digital dollar, but it is not the same kind of stablecoin as USDC or USDT. Ethena backs USDe with assets and uses offsetting short positions and other reserve strategies to control risk and generate returns.

What does it actually do?

Ethena issues USDe and sUSDe. Protocol backing can generate revenue from sources such as hedged futures trades, lending, tokenized real-world assets and stablecoin holdings. USDe users can use it across exchanges and DeFi.

Why does ENA exist?

ENA is Ethena's governance token. Holders help govern things such as what backs USDe, new backing strategies, future protocol-revenue allocation and the Risk Committee. ENA can also be staked into sENA.

How was ENA created?

ENA launched with 15 billion tokens. The published allocation was 30% core contributors, 25% investors, 15% Foundation and 30% ecosystem development/airdrops. Team and investor allocations used long vesting schedules rather than becoming liquid all at once.

The simple bull case: If USDe becomes a major digital dollar used across exchanges and DeFi, Ethena becomes more important. That can make governance, staking and any approved value-accrual mechanisms around ENA more valuable.
The simple risk: ENA is not USDe. Owning ENA means you are betting on the value of the Ethena ecosystem/governance token. Ethena also carries special risks from hedging, exchanges/counterparties, smart contracts, funding rates and regulation. USDe itself is explicitly not the same as a fiat-backed stablecoin.
